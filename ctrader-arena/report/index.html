Deriv demo · cTrader · $30 account · minimum lot · M1–M15 · report of 13 Nov 2026

Four agents, about 5,100 backtests, no system that earns a place on the account

Four research agents competed to build a cBot with a profit factor of at least 1.7 that trades every day on a $30 Deriv account. None qualified. Three systems looked good on the recent two months; each one that could be checked against earlier months failed there.

Supervisor verdict Nothing is approved for a demo forward test as a trading system.
  • Best honest result: DELTA's S&P 500 fade, profit factor 1.43 on the scored window and 1.47 on the 5.5 months before it. It is real but modest, comes from one symbol, and did not replicate on a sister index.
  • Highest scores are not real: ALPHA's 2.04 and CHARLIE's 2.30 both fell to about 1.0 on earlier data.
  • One open question: BRAVO's Drift Switch system swings from 2.82 to 0.66 depending only on the spread assumed. Recording live bid and ask prices for a few days would settle it.

Which gates each system passed

The 1.7 target alone is easy to hit by accident on a short window, so a system also had to hold up on unseen days, under wider spreads, and with enough trades to mean something.

Two results that would have fooled a simpler test

ALPHA: the London breakout, month by month

Earlier months (M5 bars, rule frozen)Scored window
The rule buys or sells the first break of the overnight range on EURUSD and GBPUSD after 08:00 London. Over January to 20 September it made 285 trades at profit factor 0.95, with a $47.57 drawdown that would have emptied a $30 account. The scored window caught seven favourable weeks.

BRAVO: Drift Switch 30 profit factor against the spread assumed

Same trades, same rules; only the assumed spread changes. Live samples ranged from 1.48 to 9.05 price units within 22 minutes, and averaged 5.8 at the moments the system wanted to buy. The engine assumes one constant spread, so a single quiet sample produces a system that passes all 12 gates and would likely lose in live trading.

How the test worked, and its limits

  • Data: real Deriv candles pulled through the cTrader connection, about two months of 1-minute bars per traded symbol, plus up to nine months of 5- and 15-minute bars for the earlier-month checks.
  • Engine: spread charged on every trade; when a bar touches both stop and target, the stop is assumed hit; spike instruments fill stops at the worst price of the bar; zero commission, matching the account's deal history.
  • Rounds: each agent submitted, was re-scored and criticised, and was sent back. ALPHA and CHARLIE had three rounds, DELTA two, BRAVO one.
  • Not modelled: overnight financing, slippage on market orders, and changing spreads. The last one decides the Drift Switch result.
  • cBots are untested: all four C# cBots are written but none has been compiled or run in cTrader, because the research environment has no cTrader toolchain.
  • Spread sampling is thin: most spreads were sampled near the daily rollover; EURUSD and GBPUSD were re-sampled in the London morning (1.2 and 1.4 pips).
  • Two engine flaws were found and fixed during the run: stop-entry orders filling mid-spike on spike instruments (found by CHARLIE), and Sunday-evening stubs counting as trading days.

Recommended next steps

  • Do not fund any of these four systems. ALPHA's and CHARLIE's files are kept only as a record and are marked rejected.
  • Settle the Drift Switch question cheaply. Run a recording-only cBot on Drift Switch 10, 20 and 30 for several days to log bid and ask on every tick. If the spread at buy signals stays under about 3.5, the system is worth a real forward test; at 5.8 it is break-even.
  • Optional observation run: DELTA's S&P 500 bot at 0.1 unit on demo, to see whether 1.3–1.5 holds. Its worst trade is capped at $1.40.
  • Reconsider the constraints. A profit factor of 1.7, trades every day, 1–15 minute signals and $30 at minimum lot could not all be met at once here. Dropping the daily-trade requirement, allowing longer holds, or a larger balance each widens the field.
