04Test 1 · Does Deriv's price lag the exchanges?

Minute by minute

I compared 1,000 one-minute Deriv BTCUSD bars (16.7 hours) with Binance and Coinbase over the same minutes. If Deriv lagged by several seconds, part of each exchange move would show up in Deriv's next minute.

Same-minute correlation is 0.994 with both exchanges. A regression of Deriv's moves on this minute's and last minute's exchange moves implies a lag of about 0.4 s. There's no minute-scale lag to trade.

Quote by quote

For finer resolution I took 26 individual Deriv quotes, each stamped to the millisecond by Deriv's server, and lined them up against exchange quotes I recorded at the same time. For each candidate lag I measured how well Deriv's price matched the exchange price shifted by that lag: the lower the scatter, the better the fit.

Deriv's quote sits about $1.30–$2 from each exchange. Best fits: about 0.4–0.5 s behind OKX and Kraken, and slightly ahead of Binance and Coinbase (−0.4 to −0.5 s). Each exchange stamps time with its own clock, so the honest reading is that Deriv is within half a second of the market, at most 0.5 s behind its fastest sources. With 26 matched quotes the exact figure is uncertain by a few hundred milliseconds.

07Test 4 · The 5-minute window strategy

This is the direct translation of Polymarket's 5-minute Up/Down windows. At every 5-minute boundary the rule looks at BTC's move over the last K minutes. If it was larger than Z times normal volatility, it trades 0.01 BTC with the move (momentum) or against it (fade) and closes 5 minutes later. Spread $18.42 per trade.

Data: 90 days of Binance 1-minute BTC (129,604 bars), used because Deriv's own history moves with it at 0.994 correlation. Rules were chosen on the first 70% of days and checked on the last 30%. Three variables (direction, K = 1/3/5 min, Z = 1.0/1.5/2.0) give 18 rules, and all 18 are reported.

Show all 18 rules (in-sample and out-of-sample)
No 5-minute rule has an edge. Before the spread, results hover around zero (±$0.12 per trade at most), which is what a random coin flip on BTC direction looks like. On a CFD, unlike Polymarket, there is no mispriced contract to exploit at this horizon: you are simply betting on BTC's direction.

10The bot: LatArbBot for cTrader

A cTrader Automate cBot in C#, at latarb-ctrader/LatArbBot.cs on branch claude/polymarket-btc-arbitrage-0avqt1. It runs inside cTrader on your computer, so it sees Deriv's real ticks and your real connection speed.

  1. Leading price

    Opens a WebSocket to OKX (bbo-tbt, BTC-USDT) and keeps the best bid/ask mid.

  2. Offset

    Learns the normal OKX-vs-Deriv price offset slowly (EWMA weight 0.01), only while OKX is still, so fast moves don't leak into it.

  3. Signal

    gap = (OKX − offset) − Deriv mid. Trades when |gap| ≥ Deriv spread + $5, in the gap's direction.

  4. Exit

    Closes after 3 s, or at the $60 protective stop.

  5. Limits

    One position at a time, minimum volume, 2 s cooldown, at most 30 trades a day, $1.50 daily loss limit, $4.50 kill switch, skips when the spread is over $25 or OKX is silent for 1.5 s.

  6. Paper mode (default)

    Sends no orders. Logs each signal with Deriv's real bid/ask at entry and 3 s later, and keeps a running P&L total.

Install

1. cTrader desktop → Algo → cBots → New → paste LatArbBot.cs → Build
2. Add an instance on BTCUSD, approve Full Access (needed for the OKX WebSocket)
3. Keep "Paper mode" = Yes and run it for several days, including busy US hours
4. Read the log: "PAPER close ... total +x.xx" is the running hypothetical P&L

When to consider live mode

  • At least 100 paper trades, with a clearly positive average after the spread.
  • Switch to live on the demo account first and compare real fills with the paper log.
  • Read Deriv's terms first: most CFD brokers prohibit latency arbitrage and can cancel trades or close accounts that use it.

Assumptions and limits

  • The replay models Deriv as a delayed copy of OKX. The real Deriv feed could react differently in fast markets, for example by widening its spread or pausing quotes. The $25-spread table shows how sensitive the result is.
  • The lag estimate rests on 26 matched quotes and was taken in a quiet market, so its uncertainty is a few hundred milliseconds.
  • The 5-minute test uses Binance prices as a stand-in for Deriv history (0.994 correlation measured), with Deriv's spread charged on every trade.
  • No swap or overnight financing is modelled; every hold here is seconds to minutes.
