POLYMARKET · BTC UP OR DOWN · 5 & 15 MIN · TESTED 13–16 NOV 2026

polyarb Test Report

A Rust bot that buys Polymarket's Bitcoin Up/Down shares when their price lags the real BTC price, then sells once the book catches up. This report covers every test run, what broke and was fixed, what the results mean, and what you need to trade it with real money.

All profit and loss below is paper trading: live market data and the real order book, with simulated fills. No real order has been placed.

03How the markets work

Every 5 and 15 minutes Polymarket lists "Bitcoin Up or Down". An Up share pays $1 if BTC ends the window at or above where it started; otherwise a Down share pays $1. Shares trade between 1¢ and 99¢. The price of Up is the market's estimate of the probability BTC finishes higher.

They settle on an average price, not the last price

The market rules name Chainlink's BTC/USD TWAP (time-weighted average price) as the source. I recorded both Chainlink streams for 70 minutes and tested five candidate rules against Polymarket's official results on 20 windows.

The "price to beat" and close price shown on the website are spot prints, and comparing them picked the wrong winner in 3 of 20 windows. The bot uses the TWAP rule, and its own settlement agreed with Polymarket on every window it traded through.

Fees

Polymarket charges takers fee = shares × 0.07 × p × (1 − p) on crypto markets. The market's own data confirms it (feeSchedule: rate 0.07, exponent 1, takerOnly). Every bot trade is a taker trade, and the fee is included in every result here.

Order limits: minimum 5 shares per order, 1¢ price steps. The bot also enforces a $1 minimum order value. The 5-share minimum is what makes a $15 account hard (§8).

04Test log

Testing ran in three phases: an offline simulation on 13 Nov, then live data from 16 Nov, where several real problems showed up and were fixed, then paper trading.

05Live paper results

$1,000 paper balance, default settings (about $5 per trade). Each order filled against the real order book as it stood after the delay shown. Runs B, C and D ran at the same time on the same data, so fill delay is the only difference between them.

Slower fills miss far more orders: 13% at 80 ms, 76% at 1.5 s. At 1.5 s the bot still earned about 40% of what it did at 80 ms in this sample. Speed matters, but the edge did not vanish at home-connection speeds.

  • $15 on the tweet's rules cannot trade. Every signal was skipped: the cheapest allowed order costs dollars, the budget was 7¢.
  • $15 at 20% per trade nearly quadrupled on paper in 38 minutes because each win raised the next trade size, from about $3 to $7. It also fell 27.9% from its high, lost 5 trades in a row and hit its daily cap once. If the real edge is smaller than paper, the same compounding works in reverse just as fast.

Do not read +269% as a forecast. It is a short paper run compounding an edge that live trading will not fully reproduce (§10).

08Capital analysis

The binding constraint is Polymarket's 5-share minimum order. At the real entry prices above, the smallest allowed order (5 shares, one tick of slippage, plus fee) cost a median of $2.54 (90% of the time under $3.82, at most $4.81). The bot only trades when that fits inside risk_per_trade × balance.

GoalMinimum balance
Tweet rules (0.5% per trade) on about half of signals~$500
Tweet rules on every signal~$1,000
Moderate rules (5% per trade, 15% per day), every signal~$100
Trades at all (20% per trade, 50% per day)$15, high risk

What 20% per trade means on $15

  • Each trade risks about $2.50–$3.00, roughly 17–20% of the account.
  • A sold loser lost about 18% of its premium on average; a trade held to expiry can lose all of it.
  • Three losses in a row leave about $9–10. At that size most signals become unaffordable and the bot effectively stops.
  • Even if the paper edge held, a winning trade earns about $0.30–$0.40.

The tweet's claims

"$400–$700 a day" at 0.5% risk per trade would need an account in the tens of thousands of dollars, each trade taking more liquidity than the book often has. "$50 → $435,000" and "1,000 orders per second" don't fit Polymarket's order minimums, fees or API, and the dashboard in the video contradicts its own numbers.

09Simulation

An offline test with a synthetic BTC path and a simulated market maker whose quotes trail spot by a fixed delay. It runs the same engine, with Polymarket's real fee curve and TWAP rule. It tests the mechanics, not profitability, because the simulated market maker never adapts.

A separate test fed the bot a deliberately wrong volatility: losses stopped at the 2% daily cap, confirming the risk limit works.

10Why live results will be worse than paper

A mean of +12.5% per trade over an 8-second hold is far better than a real market should allow. Expect live results somewhere between a fraction of paper and a loss. Only real fills will tell.

Paper assumesLive reality
Nobody else takes the stale quoteOther bots run the same idea; when they get there first you fill worse or not at all
The book the bot sees is currentWebSocket updates arrive after the fact; some "stale" quotes are already gone
Orders match immediatelyPolymarket can delay marketable orders (delayed status), which is exactly what defeats a latency strategy
Exits sell into the full displayed bidBids thin out or get pulled the moment BTC moves
One quiet morning is typicalVolatile markets bring bigger moves, wider books and more trades stuck to expiry
80–1,500 ms delay from a data centreA home connection adds more

12Going live, step by step

  1. Check your location

    On the computer you'll trade from, open https://polymarket.com/api/geoblock. If it says "blocked":true, stop here.

  2. Build and paper-trade for at least a day

    git clone https://github.com/brainkitt/treasure.git
cd treasure && git checkout claude/polymarket-btc-arbitrage-0avqt1
cd polymarket-arb
cargo build --release
cp config.example.toml config.toml
./target/release/polyarb run          # open http://127.0.0.1:8787

    Confirm every feed shows a fresh time, markets appear, and a strike is set within seconds of each window opening.

  3. Choose the wallet

    You haveUseConfig
    Nothing yet, or a polymarket.com account made after 4 May 2026A new plain wallet used only for thissignature_type = 0
    polymarket.com account made before 4 May 2026, email/Google loginThat account's exported signing keysignature_type = 1 + funder
    polymarket.com account made before 4 May 2026, MetaMask/RabbyThat browser wallet's keysignature_type = 2 + funder

  4. Fund it

    Plain-wallet route: send USDC.e and a little POL to the wallet on the Polygon network.

  5. Approve tokens (plain wallet only)

    export POLYMARKET_PRIVATE_KEY=0x...        # your own terminal only
./target/release/polyarb approve --check   # shows POL, USDC.e and approvals
./target/release/polyarb approve           # sends the two approval transactions

    Set POLYGON_RPC_URL to another Polygon endpoint if the default is slow.

  6. Set the config

    Set bankroll to exactly your wallet balance and pick a profile from §13.

  7. First live session, supervised

    ./target/release/polyarb run --live

    Check the first lines: authenticated as 0x… is your wallet; wallet collateral balance $X matches your deposit (stop if it reads $0); no no exchange allowance warning. Compare the first EXEC and FILLED lines with your positions on Polymarket. Ctrl-C stops the bot; open positions remain and resolve on their own.

  8. Redeem winners

    After a window ends, redeem any winning shares still held so the money is usable again.

15Open items before real money

  1. First real orders

    Never sent. Expect at least one small fix after seeing real responses.

  2. Deposit Wallet support

    Needs the executor ported to Polymarket's official TypeScript or Python client, or a Rust SDK update.

  3. Automatic redemption

    For winning shares held to expiry.

  4. Authenticated price feed

    Migrate before Polymarket switches off the public stream.

  5. A longer paper record

    Several days covering quiet and volatile markets, at your real latency.
